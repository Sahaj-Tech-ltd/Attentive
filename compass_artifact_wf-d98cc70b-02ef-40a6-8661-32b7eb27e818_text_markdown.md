# The neuropsychological science behind building an ADHD assessment tool

**An open-source ADHD self-assessment screener is scientifically feasible if built on validated constructs, but requires careful implementation grounded in signal detection theory, working memory research, and responsible clinical framing.** The core insight from this research is that no single test diagnoses ADHD — formal assessment works through converging evidence across self-report, cognitive performance, and behavioral observation. The six components in a standard battery each probe different neuropsychological constructs (sustained attention, inhibitory control, working memory, processing speed, fluid reasoning), and their combined pattern is what yields diagnostic confidence. For an open-source tool, the ASRS v1.1 (freely available from WHO) provides the self-report backbone, a web-based CPT provides the strongest objective measure, and additional cognitive tasks add convergent validity — all framed explicitly as a screener, never a diagnosis.

## 1. Self-report rating scales: what they measure and which are free

Self-report is the entry point to any ADHD evaluation. The theory is straightforward: only the individual can report on internal experiences like racing thoughts, difficulty sustaining concentration, or emotional dysregulation. Rating scales translate DSM symptom criteria into quantifiable Likert-scale items, normed against population samples to produce T-scores (mean=50, SD=10) or percentile rankings.

**The ASRS v1.1** (Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale) is the only major validated instrument available for free use. Developed by the WHO in collaboration with Harvard Medical School (Kessler, Adler, Spencer), it contains **18 items mapping to the 18 DSM symptom criteria** across two domains: Inattention (9 items) and Hyperactivity-Impulsivity (9 items). The 6-item Part A screener uses the most diagnostically predictive items. Under the updated February 2024 scoring (Likert 0–4, range 0–24), a cutoff of **≥14 yields sensitivity of 90% and specificity of 88%**, with an AUC of 0.90. Factor analysis (Stanton et al., 2018, N>1,000) actually reveals three factors rather than two: Inattentiveness, Motor Hyperactivity/Impulsivity, and Verbal Hyperactivity. The ASRS-5, updated for DSM-5, achieved even better operating characteristics in the Ustun et al. (2017) JAMA Psychiatry validation: **sensitivity 91.4%, specificity 96.0%, AUC 0.94**. The simple dimensional scoring (0–24) is freely usable; only the proprietary optimized weighted scoring requires institutional agreement through NYU.

The other major instruments are all proprietary. **CAARS** (Conners Adult ADHD Rating Scales; Multi-Health Systems) provides 8 subscales including an ADHD Index and Inconsistency Index across 66 items, with T-scores stratified by age and gender. **Brown EF/A Scales** (Pearson) uniquely model ADHD as executive function disorder across 6 clusters — Activation, Focus, Effort, Emotion, Memory, Action — reflecting Thomas Brown's theory that ADHD is fundamentally about impaired self-management of cognitive systems rather than purely behavioral symptoms. **BRIEF-A** (PAR Inc.) assesses 9 executive function domains producing a Global Executive Composite, with **T≥65 as the clinical threshold**. The Working Memory, Plan/Organize, and Inhibit subscales are most discriminating for ADHD.

A critical limitation of self-report in ADHD is the **Positive Illusory Bias**: adults with ADHD tend to underreport severity and overestimate competence. Self-report persistence rates are approximately half of informant-report rates. Heritability estimates based on self-report (38–48%) are substantially lower than those from parent-report (64–82%), suggesting self-report introduces significant measurement noise. Additionally, 25–50% of university students seeking ADHD assessment may feign symptoms. For the open-source tool, this means self-report should be combined with performance-based measures and include validity indicators (inconsistency checks, response pattern analysis).

**For implementation**: Use the ASRS v1.1 as the primary self-report component (free, validated, well-normed). Add the **Wender Utah Rating Scale (WURS-25)** for retrospective childhood symptom assessment (cutoff 46: sensitivity 86%, specificity 99%). Supplement with custom functional impairment items derived from DSM-5 criteria (behavioral descriptions are not copyrightable). Include brief depression (PHQ-2) and anxiety (GAD-2) screens to flag differential diagnosis concerns.

## 2. Visuospatial construction tasks probe planning and spatial working memory

The "cube/shape, make a triangle from edges" task in the assessment is analogous to **Block Design** from the WAIS-IV, the prototype test for visuospatial ability. The test-taker uses red-and-white blocks to replicate patterns shown on stimulus cards, scored by both accuracy and speed. Block Design loads on the **Perceptual Reasoning Index (PRI)** in WAIS-IV (Visual Spatial Index in WAIS-V) and measures visuospatial processing, visual-motor coordination, executive planning, and working memory simultaneously — the test-taker must parse the target design, hold components in visuospatial working memory, and execute a sequential construction plan.

The neuropsychological rationale for including visuospatial tasks in ADHD batteries rests on a robust finding: **spatial working memory deficits in ADHD are substantially larger than verbal deficits**. The foundational Martinussen et al. (2005) meta-analysis found effect sizes of **d=0.85 for spatial storage and d=1.06 for spatial central executive functions**, compared to d=0.47 for verbal storage. More recent bifactor modeling (Kofler et al., 2020) found central executive working memory impairments reaching **d=1.63–2.03, with 75–81% of ADHD participants impaired**. These visuospatial working memory deficits are more closely tied to ADHD-related hyperactivity, disinhibition, and attention problems than phonological deficits.

Block Design is particularly revealing in ADHD because of its timed component — processing speed under visuospatial demand exposes the PSI deficits characteristic of ADHD. Qualitative observations during construction tasks provide additional clinical data: impulsive starts without adequate planning, excessive trial-and-error rather than strategic placement, and disorganized assembly approaches. **Visual Puzzles** (also WAIS-IV) removes the motor component entirely — comparing VP and BD scores helps parse whether low performance reflects genuine visuospatial deficits versus processing speed limitations, a critical distinction in ADHD assessment.

**For implementation**: A web-based analog could use a grid-based drag-and-rotate system where users arrange colored squares to match target patterns, with progressive difficulty scaling (2×2 → 3×3 → 4×4 grids). Record both accuracy and detailed process data: number of moves, hesitations, corrections, and time per placement. Existing open-source resources include the **twpride/tangram** GitHub repository (179 tangram puzzles, JavaScript/Canvas, timed scoring) and **PEBL** (Psychology Experiment Building Language, includes Corsi Block-Tapping and Mental Rotation tasks, now browser-compatible via WebAssembly). PsyToolkit offers a Corsi Block Test implementation for visuospatial working memory.

## 3. Timed math exercises tax working memory at its breaking point

Timed math in a neuropsychological battery corresponds to the **WAIS-IV Arithmetic subtest**, which loads on the Working Memory Index. The examinee solves oral word problems mentally, without paper, under time pressure — a triple demand on holding numerical information in working memory, executing calculations, and managing time. This subtest is part of the historically sensitive **ACID profile** (Arithmetic, Coding, Information, Digit Span) and **SCAD profile** (Symbol Search, Coding, Arithmetic, Digit Span), both identified as characteristic of ADHD.

The theoretical foundation lies in Barkley's updated model of ADHD, which elevated working memory from a mediator variable to **a primary exogenous variable alongside behavioral inhibition**. The functional working memory model (Rapport et al., 2001, 2009) positions working memory deficits as a core feature, suggesting that hyperactivity may actually represent an attempt to increase cortical arousal needed for central executive functioning. Meta-analytic evidence is striking: when tests adequately measure the "working" components of working memory, **75–85% of youth with ADHD show impairments** (Kasper et al., 2012, meta-regression: d=2.01–2.15). In over 700 children with ADHD, **90% received their lowest scores on WMI and PSI domains** (Mayes & Calhoun, 2006).

**Processing speed** is measured separately through tasks like Coding (copying digit-symbol pairs) and Symbol Search (scanning for target symbols), comprising the Processing Speed Index. PSI deficits are particularly prominent in **ADHD-Predominantly Inattentive type**. Kibby et al. (2019) found that while simple reaction time is intact in ADHD, processing speed becomes less efficient as task demands increase — a "processing speed as multifaceted system" model. The "pure" inattentive subgroup (≤2 hyperactive-impulsive symptoms) shows the slowest performance, particularly on set-shifting speed tasks, with effect sizes double those of the broader ADHD-PI group (Goth-Owens et al., 2010).

Timed math specifically combines working memory load with processing speed demands — the two core ADHD deficit areas. Time pressure creates dual demands: cognitive task performance plus time monitoring, the latter tapping into ADHD's well-documented poor temporal processing. Harrison et al. (2024) advise that substituting Letter-Number Sequencing for Arithmetic may be preferable when assessing ADHD, given high rates of comorbid math learning disorders that can artificially lower WMI scores.

**For implementation**: Create progressive difficulty levels from single-digit addition through multi-digit operations to word problems, with time constraints mirroring clinical standards (15–75 seconds per item depending on complexity). Record accuracy, response latency, and items attempted within time limits. Include both simple calculations (processing speed proxy) and complex working-memory-loaded problems. The PASAT (Paced Auditory Serial Addition Test) offers an excellent model — serial addition under controlled intervals taxes continuous working memory updating. Inquisit offers a free computerized PASAT; PsyToolkit supports creating timed arithmetic experiments.

## 4. Two types of pattern recognition test fluid reasoning and conceptual abstraction

The two pattern recognition tasks in a formal ADHD battery correspond to **Matrix Reasoning** (visual/nonverbal) and **Similarities** (verbal/conceptual) from the WAIS-IV — testing fluid reasoning across modalities.

**Matrix Reasoning** presents incomplete visual matrices where the examinee selects the pattern-completing response from multiple choices. It measures fluid reasoning (Gf) — reasoning with novel stimuli independent of prior knowledge — loading on the Perceptual Reasoning Index (WAIS-IV) or Fluid Reasoning Index (WAIS-V). An fMRI study found that children with ADHD demonstrated **hypoactivation in regions critical for fluid reasoning** including the intraparietal sulcus (visual attention/spatial processing) and cerebellum (cognitive planning, abstract reasoning, attentional shifting). The connection is that working memory, attention, inhibition, and interference resolution are all prerequisites for successful fluid reasoning, and all are deficit areas in ADHD.

**Similarities** asks "In what way are X and Y alike?" and scores responses 0–2 based on level of abstraction (concrete → functional → abstract/categorical). It loads on the Verbal Comprehension Index and tests crystallized intelligence plus abstract verbal reasoning. VCI is typically a relative strength in ADHD, yet a Turkish WISC-IV study found that Similarities, Matrix Reasoning, and Digit Span together **classified 83% of children as having ADHD** — suggesting that even verbal reasoning tasks tap executive-attentional demands through their abstract reasoning requirement.

Both types are assessed because the discrepancy between verbal and visual reasoning informs differential diagnosis. ADHD can differentially impact processing across modalities depending on subtype and comorbidities. The combined assessment provides convergent validity: visual pattern tasks reveal visuospatial working memory and processing speed deficits; verbal pattern tasks reveal language-mediated executive dysfunction. The broader D-KEFS battery (Delis-Kaplan Executive Function System) adds process-level analysis: Trail Making (cognitive flexibility), Color-Word Interference (response inhibition via Stroop-like paradigm), and Tower Test (spatial planning) — with ADHD showing significant deficits on inhibition and planning measures, while **24.3% of ADHD patients meeting criteria for executive function deficit** on D-KEFS.

The characteristic "scattered" ADHD cognitive profile shows preserved VCI and PRI relative to depressed WMI and PSI. However, Watkins (2003) and others caution that subtest scatter has limited individual diagnostic accuracy — contributing only 2–8% variance beyond general ability. The ACID/SCAD profiles should serve as confirmatory evidence, not sole diagnostic criteria.

**For implementation**: Matrix-type tasks are straightforward to implement as web-based multiple-choice pattern completion using SVG or Canvas rendering. Verbal similarities can be implemented as text-based prompts with open-ended response scoring (though automated scoring of verbal responses is more challenging). PsyToolkit offers a Wisconsin Card Sorting Test for cognitive flexibility, and jsPsych supports building Flanker and Stroop tasks for executive function measurement.

## 5. The CPT is the strongest objective measure, and HRT ISI Change is its most diagnostic signal

The Continuous Performance Test sits at the heart of objective ADHD assessment. The Conners CPT-3 (the clinical gold standard) uses the **non-X paradigm**: respond to every letter except X across **360 trials in 6 blocks** with ISIs varying at 1, 2, and 4 seconds. The high response rate (80% targets in CPT-3) establishes a prepotent motor response that must be inhibited on No-Go trials — specifically taxing response inhibition, the core executive deficit in Barkley's model.

### Signal detection theory provides the mathematical framework

CPT scoring rests on **signal detection theory** (Green & Swets, 1966), which decomposes performance into discriminability and response bias:

**d-prime (d')** = z(Hit Rate) − z(False Alarm Rate), where z() is the inverse normal CDF. This represents the standardized distance between signal and noise distributions — higher d' means better ability to discriminate targets from non-targets. ADHD shows significantly lower d' with large effect sizes (Huang-Pollock et al., 2012 meta-analysis). The critical implementation detail: when Hit Rate = 1.0 or FA Rate = 0, z-scores become ±infinity. The **log-linear correction** (Hautus, 1995) handles this: Hit Rate = (hits + 0.5)/(signal trials + 1); FA Rate = (false alarms + 0.5)/(noise trials + 1).

**Criterion c** = −0.5 × [z(Hit Rate) + z(False Alarm Rate)], measuring response bias. ADHD patients tend toward a **liberal response bias** (c < 0) — a lower threshold for responding, producing more commission errors. Epstein et al. (2003) found that both d' and β were "highly related to all ADHD symptoms across symptom domains" rather than showing domain specificity.

### Six metrics matter, but three are paramount

**HRT Standard Deviation** is one of the most ubiquitous and sensitive neuropsychological findings in ADHD. Response time variability reflects attentional lapses — occasional very slow responses inflate the standard deviation. A meta-analysis of 319 studies confirmed **RT variability is more consistently elevated in ADHD than mean RT**. The mechanism maps to the Default Mode Network theory: inadequate suppression of DMN activity during task performance causes attentional lapses that appear as the slow tail in the RT distribution. This is best captured by the **ex-Gaussian τ (tau) parameter** — when RT distributions are decomposed into a normal component (μ, σ) and exponential component (τ), ADHD individuals show larger τ values, meaning more extremely slow responses. Critically, when the exponential contribution is factored out, ADHD individuals actually respond *faster* (smaller μ) than controls — the deficit is in consistency, not speed.

**HRT ISI Change** is often the single most clinically significant CPT finding for ADHD. The three ISI conditions (1s, 2s, 4s) create different arousal demands: 1-second ISI provides external pacing (easier), while 4-second ISI requires internal effort regulation (harder). ADHD individuals perform near-normally at fast event rates but show **marked RT slowing at slow event rates** — reflecting inability to maintain optimal arousal under low stimulation. This maps directly to Sergeant's Cognitive-Energetic Model (deficit in "energetic pools" of arousal, activation, and effort) and Zentall's Optimal Stimulation Theory (higher threshold for optimal arousal). The ISI effect is direction-specific to ADHD: persons with schizophrenia-related inattention perform *best* at 4-second ISI, creating an important differential diagnostic pattern. The ex-Gaussian τ difference between ADHD and controls increases as ISI increases, making the longest ISI the most diagnostically powerful condition.

**Omissions** (missed targets) reflect sustained attention failure — the inattention signature. **Commissions** (false alarms to X) reflect impulsivity. But the nuance matters: high commissions + fast RT = impulsivity; high commissions + slow RT = inattentiveness (not attending to stimulus identity). **HRT Block Change** captures vigilance decrement across the ~14-minute administration — ADHD shows greater deterioration in later blocks. **Perseverations** (RT < 100ms) indicate anticipatory/random responding.

### T-score computation and interpretation

Raw CPT scores convert to T-scores using age- and gender-stratified normative tables from the Conners CPT-3 normative sample (N=1,400, representative of U.S. population across 8 age groups). The formula is: T = 50 + 10 × [(Raw Score − Normative Mean) / Normative SD]. Direction matters: for Omissions, Commissions, HRT SD, and Variability, higher T = worse. For d', the scale is inverted so low T = worse discrimination. The interpretation framework: **T 41–59 average; T 60–64 mildly atypical; T 65–69 moderately atypical; T ≥70 markedly atypical**. The CPT-3 reports composites: Inattentiveness (d', Omissions, Commissions, HRT, HRT SD, Variability), Impulsivity (HRT, Commissions, Perseverations), Sustained Attention (HRT Block Change + SE), and Vigilance (HRT ISI Change + SE).

### Web implementation requires careful timing

The critical implementation constraint is timing precision. `performance.now()` (sub-millisecond, monotonic) must be used over `Date.now()` (millisecond-only, non-monotonic). For stimulus presentation, a **continuous requestAnimationFrame loop** (Lukács & Gaspar, 2022, Behavior Research Methods) provides "practically faultless precision" — significantly better than single rAF calls or setTimeout. Browser security features coarsen `performance.now()` to ~2ms in Firefox; Cross-Origin isolation headers restore higher precision. For CPT purposes where relevant RT differences are 50–200ms between conditions, **±5ms precision is adequate**. Trial-level data should capture: trial index, block, sub-block, ISI, stimulus, whether target, response given, reaction time, and timestamps for stimulus onset and response.

**Available open-source CPT implementations**: the ConjunctiveContinuousPerformanceTask repository (GitHub, includes jsPsych plugin), PsyToolkit's AX-CPT and SART experiments, the PsychoPy-based ContinuousPerformanceTest repository, and PEBL's CPT implementation. jsPsych is the most mature web-based framework for building a custom CPT with its `html-keyboard-response` plugin.

## 6. Integrating components into a composite ADHD probability score

No universally accepted formal algorithm exists for ADHD diagnosis from neuropsychological batteries. Clinical decision-making uses a **converging evidence approach** — pattern analysis across multiple sources rather than reliance on any single test. The clinical interview carries the highest diagnostic weight (the DIVA-5 structured interview detected ADHD more accurately than neuropsychological testing or rating scales in Pettersson et al., 2018). Self-report questionnaires provide symptom severity but are subjective. CPTs alone correctly classified only **51.7% of confirmed adult ADHD patients** (Baggio et al., 2020), with meta-analytic AUCs "barely acceptable" (0.7–0.8) — but classification improves substantially when CPT results are combined with rating scale data.

The most promising integration method is **multi-stage Bayesian updating**, consistent with how clinicians naturally reason. Each piece of evidence updates the prior probability:

- **Base rate** (prior probability): ADHD prevalence ~2–5% in adults in general population, but ~30–50% in self-referred clinical populations seeking ADHD evaluation
- **Self-report questionnaire** contributes a likelihood ratio (ASRS Part A ≥14: LR+ ≈ 7.5, LR− ≈ 0.11)
- **CPT performance** contributes additional likelihood ratios based on profile pattern
- **Cognitive test pattern** (low WMI/PSI relative to VCI/PRI) provides further updating

Machine learning approaches have achieved strong results: XGBoost combining parent reports, self-reports, and neuropsych measures achieved **AUC = 0.906** (accuracy 82.4%); Random Forest using CPT data alone achieved 87% accuracy; a multi-stage Bayesian classifier predicted expert consensus with >86% accuracy across two large cohorts. The key predictive features identified across studies include RT variability (ex-Gaussian τ), parent-rated executive dysfunction (inhibition, working memory), and self-regulation traits.

**The base rate problem is critical to communicate to users**: even a test with 90% sensitivity and 90% specificity, applied to a 5% base-rate population, yields a positive predictive value of only ~32%. The tool must present results as probability ranges — "Very Unlikely" (<15%), "Unlikely" (15–30%), "Possible" (30–50%), "Likely" (50–70%), "Very Likely" (>70%) — never as binary diagnosis. Including brief screens for depression (PHQ-2), anxiety (GAD-2), and sleep quality is essential because these conditions mimic ADHD on testing, and ~2/3 of people with ADHD have at least one comorbid condition.

## The ADHD-Inattentive cognitive signature is distinct and measurable

**ADHD-Predominantly Inattentive presentation** (DSM-5 314.00, ICD-10 F90.0) is the most common presentation in adults and females, yet paradoxically under-recognized. Its neuropsychological signature is a **GAI > CPI discrepancy** — the General Ability Index (VCI + PRI) exceeds the Cognitive Proficiency Index (WMI + PSI), indicating that reasoning ability is higher than cognitive efficiency. In the Pearson WAIS-IV ADHD clinical study (N=44), the largest effect size was for **CPI: 93.6 vs. 100.5 (d=0.53)**, while VCI showed minimal difference (d=0.12).

Processing speed deficits are the hallmark. ADHD-PI shows lower PSI than ADHD-Combined on some measures, with "pure" inattentive cases (≤2 hyperactive-impulsive symptoms) exhibiting the slowest performance. This overlaps with **Cognitive Disengagement Syndrome** (CDS, formerly Sluggish Cognitive Tempo) — excessive daydreaming, mental confusion, slowed behavior — present in 30–63% of ADHD-PI cases. The CDS neurocognitive phenotype is specifically characterized by slowed cognitive processing on any task with a timed component, regardless of domain.

On CPT, the ADHD-I profile shows elevated omissions, elevated HRT SD, and significant HRT ISI Change (vigilance decay), with fewer commissions than combined type. Egeland et al. (2009) found a clean dissociation: **ADHD-Combined showed poorer vigilance** (HRT ISI Change) while **ADHD-Inattentive showed poorer sustained attention** (HRT Block Change). This differential pattern is diagnostically useful but CPT classification accuracy for subtype identification remains limited (~80%).

The neurobiological basis centers on prefrontal cortex dysfunction. The dorsolateral PFC is critical for working memory, and adults with ADHD show reduced activation during working memory tasks. ADHD is associated with impaired catecholamine (dopamine, norepinephrine) signaling in PFC, genetic changes that slow PFC maturation, and — crucially — **failure to suppress the Default Mode Network during task performance**. The DMN (medial PFC, posterior cingulate) remains active when it should deactivate, creating a "persistent magnetic pull away from the task at hand." Stimulant medications work by optimizing catecholamine levels in PFC, improving signal-to-noise ratio, and robustly reversing DMN-like functional connectivity intrusions during cognitive tasks.

## Ethical and legal framing determines whether this tool helps or harms

Under the **2026 FDA Clinical Decision Support guidance** (January 6, 2026), non-diagnostic informational tools that present results transparently and recommend professional follow-up likely qualify for exemption from medical device regulation. The guidance extended enforcement discretion for software producing risk scores, removed language excluding probability outputs from exemption, and signaled broader deregulation of digital health tools. However, the EU Medical Device Regulation (2017/745) is more stringent — software providing diagnosis-related information may be classified as a medical device under Rule 11.

The essential design principles for responsible implementation are:

- **Frame as educational screener**: Every results screen must state "This tool is a screening instrument only. It does not provide a clinical diagnosis."
- **Use categorical presentation, not raw scores**: A traffic-light system (Low Concern / Moderate Concern / High Concern) is far more appropriate for lay users than T-scores or d-prime values, which are easily misinterpreted
- **Zero data retention architecture**: Process all data client-side in the browser. Collect no personally identifiable information. This sidesteps HIPAA and GDPR entirely
- **Include differential diagnosis flags**: Brief depression, anxiety, and sleep screens with language like "Some of your responses suggest symptoms that may be better explained by [condition]. A professional evaluation can help distinguish these."
- **Acknowledge known limitations**: Browser timing variability, environmental distractions, absence of clinical observation, and the inherent limitations of self-administered testing
- **Provide crisis resources**: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, given ADHD's comorbidity with depression
- **APA Standard 9** (Assessment) requires that assessment instruments be used for purposes supported by research evidence, and results explained in understandable language. The International Test Commission guidelines on internet-based testing emphasize evidence of technical adequacy, equivalence demonstration, and fairness/accessibility

## Conclusion: a scientifically grounded architecture for the tool

The viable open-source implementation path uses **ASRS v1.1 as the self-report backbone** (free, WHO-authorized, AUC 0.90), a **custom web-based CPT built with jsPsych** (using requestAnimationFrame loop for timing, computing d-prime, HRT SD, and HRT ISI Change client-side), and supplementary cognitive tasks drawn from PsyToolkit's experiment library (Corsi Block Test for visuospatial WM, SART for sustained attention) or custom-built timed arithmetic and pattern recognition tasks. The signal detection theory scoring algorithms (d-prime, criterion c) are public domain formulas; published normative data from Adler et al. (2019, N=22,397) provides ASRS population norms; and the general T-score formula (T = 50 + 10 × [(raw − mean)/SD]) is universally applicable given published means and standard deviations.

The deepest insight from this research is that **HRT ISI Change and HRT SD are more diagnostically powerful than traditional error rates** — yet most online ADHD tools focus only on omissions and commissions. A tool that implements variable ISI conditions and computes reaction time variability metrics would capture what makes formal CPTs clinically valuable. The Bayesian integration framework — starting from an appropriate base rate, then sequentially updating with self-report likelihood ratios, CPT profile patterns, and cognitive test results — mirrors clinical reasoning and produces calibrated probability estimates rather than false diagnostic certainty. The difference between a responsible screener and a harmful pseudo-diagnostic tool lies entirely in this framing: what the user sees should be a probability range with explicit uncertainty, a clear recommendation for professional evaluation, and psychoeducation about what ADHD is and isn't — never a label.
